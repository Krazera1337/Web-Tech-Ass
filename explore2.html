Plant Genera

← Back to Explore

A genus sits one rank below family in plant classification: it groups species that share a very recent common ancestor and a distinctive combination of flower, fruit and vegetative features not found in neighbouring genera. This page profiles the eight genera featured on Forest Story — two nested inside each of the four families covered in the Families section. Each genus entry gives an overview of its distribution and defining traits, alongside links to the two species profiled from that genus.

Where genus sits in the hierarchy

Botanical classification narrows step by step from very broad to very specific:

  1. Kingdom (Plantae)
  2. Order (e.g. Sapindales)
  3. Family (e.g. Meliaceae)
  4. Genus (e.g. Aglaia)
  5. Species (e.g. Aglaia elliptica)

Genera of Myristicaceae

Horsfieldia

Horsfieldia is a genus of roughly 100 dioecious trees ranging from the eastern Himalayan foothills across mainland Southeast Asia to Sumatra and the Malay Peninsula. Trees typically bear obovate-to-oblanceolate leaves with prominent lateral veins and arillate capsular fruit relished by hornbills and imperial pigeons. One featured species has a large range across mainland Asia, while the other is restricted to a small pocket of the western Malay Peninsula and Sumatra.

  • Horsfieldia flocculosa
  • Horsfieldia kingii

Knema

Knema comprises around 90 species of small to medium trees found from India to New Guinea, with particular diversity in central and eastern Malesia. Its fruit is distinctive within the nutmeg family for often being covered in a persistent, fine hairy indumentum, and its membranous-to-leathery leaves frequently bear tiny scale-like hairs on the underside. Both featured species grow in wet lowland forest across the Indonesian archipelago.

  • Knema cinerea
  • Knema sumatrana

Genera of Chrysobalanaceae

Parastemon

Parastemon is a small genus of only a handful of species, restricted to Southeast Asia, where its members are major canopy trees of peat-swamp and kerangas (heath) forest. Leaves are leathery with a distinctive tapering, tail-like tip, and flowers are borne in slender racemes. One featured species is widespread across Sundaland; the other is endemic to Borneo and notably larger-fruited.

  • Parastemon urophyllus
  • Parastemon grandifructus

Hirtella

Hirtella is a Neotropical genus of around 100 shrubs and small trees found from Mexico to Brazil and Bolivia, mostly in the understorey of humid lowland forest. Flowers are small, often white or pink, with a few stamens arranged asymmetrically to one side of the flower — a signature trait of the genus. Fruit is a fleshy, single-seeded drupe eaten by monkeys and birds.

  • Hirtella triandra
  • Hirtella racemosa

Genera of Meliaceae

Aglaia

Aglaia is one of the largest genera in the mahogany family, with roughly 120 species of trees and shrubs across tropical Asia and the western Pacific. Nearly every part of the plant is covered in distinctive star-shaped (stellate) scales or hairs, and flowers are typically tiny but extremely fragrant. Fruit is an arillate capsule or berry, often edible, and several species have a long history of local cultivation for their fruit.

  • Aglaia elliptica
  • Aglaia korthalsii

Dysoxylum

Dysoxylum contains around 80 species of large forest trees ranging from South Asia through Malesia to Australia and the Pacific. Many species are important emergent canopy timber trees, with large pinnately compound leaves and flowers often borne directly on the trunk (cauliflory). Both featured species are large, currently classified in different threat categories, and produce notably large capsular fruit.

  • Dysoxylum acutangulum
  • Dysoxylum grande

Genera of Ebenaceae

Diospyros

Diospyros is a huge genus of some 700 species — the ebony genus — distributed across the tropics and subtropics of Asia, Africa and the Americas, and also the genus that gives us the cultivated persimmon. Leaves are simple and alternate, flowers are unisexual, and fruit is a berry seated in a persistent calyx. Several species, including one featured here, have historically been the primary commercial source of black ebony wood.

  • Diospyros ferrea
  • Diospyros ebenum

Euclea

Euclea is the African counterpart to Diospyros, comprising around 20 species of shrubs and small trees native to eastern and southern Africa and the Arabian Peninsula. Leaves are hard and leathery, flowers are small, cream-coloured and sweetly scented, and fruit is a small black berry eaten by birds and people alike. Both featured species have deep roots in African traditional medicine and material culture.

  • Euclea natalensis
  • Euclea racemosa

Genus-level terms

Dioecious
Having male and female flowers on separate individual plants, as in Horsfieldia and Knema.
Cauliflory
The habit of producing flowers (and later fruit) directly from the trunk or older branches, seen in some Dysoxylum species.
Congener
A species belonging to the same genus as another — for example, Hirtella triandra and Hirtella racemosa are congeners.
Type species
The species formally designated as the reference point that anchors the definition of a genus.

A tale of two continents

Two of our four families — Chrysobalanaceae and Ebenaceae — show a striking pattern where one genus evolved mainly in Asia and its closest relative evolved independently in Africa or the Americas. Comparing Parastemon with Hirtella, or Diospyros with Euclea, is a good way to see how the same family can look quite different depending on which continent it evolved on.
