Explore plant genera

A genus sits one rank below family, grouping species that share a very recent common ancestor. This page profiles the eight genera featured on Forest Story, each linking through to its species.

A genus sits one rank below family in plant classification: it groups species that share a very recent common ancestor and a distinctive combination of flower, fruit and vegetative features not found in neighbouring genera. This page profiles the eight genera featured on Forest Story — two nested inside each of the four families covered in the Families section. Each genus entry gives an overview of its distribution and defining traits, alongside links to the two species profiled from that genus.

Genera of Myristicaceae

Myristicaceae

Horsfieldia

About the genus

Horsfieldia is a genus of roughly 100 dioecious trees ranging from the eastern Himalayan foothills across mainland Southeast Asia to Sumatra and the Malay Peninsula. One featured species has a large range across mainland Asia, while the other is restricted to a small pocket of the western Malay Peninsula and Sumatra.

Key characteristics

  • Growth — Dioecious trees, roughly 100 species
  • Leaves — Obovate-to-oblanceolate, with prominent lateral veins
  • Fruit — Arillate capsular fruit relished by hornbills and imperial pigeons

Species

Horsfieldia flocculosa→ Horsfieldia kingii→
Myristicaceae

Knema

About the genus

Knema comprises around 90 species of small to medium trees found from India to New Guinea, with particular diversity in central and eastern Malesia. Both featured species grow in wet lowland forest across the Indonesian archipelago.

Key characteristics

  • Growth — Small to medium trees, roughly 90 species
  • Leaves — Membranous-to-leathery, with tiny scale-like hairs beneath
  • Fruit — Covered in a persistent, fine hairy indumentum

Species

Knema cinerea→ Knema sumatrana→

Genera of Chrysobalanaceae

Chrysobalanaceae

Parastemon

About the genus

Parastemon is a small genus of only a handful of species, restricted to Southeast Asia, where its members are major canopy trees of peat-swamp and kerangas (heath) forest. One featured species is widespread across Sundaland; the other is endemic to Borneo and notably larger-fruited.

Key characteristics

  • Growth — Major canopy trees of peat-swamp and heath forest
  • Leaves — Leathery, with a tapering, tail-like tip
  • Flowers — Borne in slender racemes

Species

Parastemon urophyllus→ Parastemon grandifructus→
Chrysobalanaceae

Hirtella

About the genus

Hirtella is a Neotropical genus of around 100 shrubs and small trees found from Mexico to Brazil and Bolivia, mostly in the understorey of humid lowland forest.

Key characteristics

  • Growth — Shrubs and small trees, roughly 100 species
  • Flowers — Small, white or pink, with asymmetrical stamens
  • Fruit — Fleshy, single-seeded drupe eaten by monkeys and birds

Species

Hirtella triandra→ Hirtella racemosa→

Genera of Meliaceae

Meliaceae

Aglaia

About the genus

Aglaia is one of the largest genera in the mahogany family, with roughly 120 species of trees and shrubs across tropical Asia and the western Pacific. Several species have a long history of local cultivation for their fruit.

Key characteristics

  • Growth — Trees and shrubs, roughly 120 species
  • Foliage — Covered in star-shaped (stellate) scales or hairs
  • Flowers — Tiny but extremely fragrant

Species

Aglaia elliptica→ Aglaia korthalsii→
Meliaceae

Dysoxylum

About the genus

Dysoxylum contains around 80 species of large forest trees ranging from South Asia through Malesia to Australia and the Pacific. Both featured species are large, currently classified in different threat categories.

Key characteristics

  • Growth — Large forest and emergent canopy timber trees
  • Leaves — Large, pinnately compound
  • Flowers — Often borne directly on the trunk (cauliflory)

Species

Dysoxylum acutangulum→ Dysoxylum grande→

Genera of Ebenaceae

Ebenaceae

Diospyros

About the genus

Diospyros is a huge genus of some 700 species — the ebony genus — distributed across the tropics and subtropics of Asia, Africa and the Americas, and also the genus that gives us the cultivated persimmon. Several species, including one featured here, have historically been the primary commercial source of black ebony wood.

Key characteristics

  • Growth — Pantropical, roughly 700 species
  • Leaves — Simple and alternate
  • Fruit — Berry seated in a persistent calyx

Species

Diospyros ferrea→ Diospyros ebenum→
Ebenaceae

Euclea

About the genus

Euclea is the African counterpart to Diospyros, comprising around 20 species of shrubs and small trees native to eastern and southern Africa and the Arabian Peninsula. Both featured species have deep roots in African traditional medicine and material culture.

Key characteristics

  • Growth — Shrubs and small trees, roughly 20 species
  • Leaves — Hard and leathery
  • Flowers — Small, cream-coloured, sweetly scented

Species

Euclea natalensis→ Euclea racemosa→

Genus-level terms

Dioecious
Having male and female flowers on separate individual plants, as in Horsfieldia and Knema.
Cauliflory
The habit of producing flowers (and later fruit) directly from the trunk or older branches, seen in some Dysoxylum species.
Congener
A species belonging to the same genus as another — for example, Hirtella triandra and Hirtella racemosa are congeners.
Type species
The species formally designated as the reference point that anchors the definition of a genus.

A tale of two continents

Two of our four families — Chrysobalanaceae and Ebenaceae — show a striking pattern where one genus evolved mainly in Asia and its closest relative evolved independently in Africa or the Americas. Comparing Parastemon with Hirtella, or Diospyros with Euclea, is a good way to see how the same family can look quite different depending on which continent it evolved on.
